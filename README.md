# Aurum

A native Android investment-wallet tracker for the US market. Kotlin + Jetpack Compose, dark premium design, no accounts and no backend — everything runs on the device.

## Features

- **Portfolio** — record buys and sells (by share count or by invested dollar amount); Aurum computes share counts, weighted average cost, realized and unrealized P/L, and day P/L from live prices. Each holding also shows its pre-market / after-hours move — the pre-market read lives while its session runs, and the after-hours read stays up overnight and across the weekend (the last extended print is the freshest information until the next session opens). The same pre/post chips appear on every pick card (daily, entries, power, weekly, under-$25). The allocation card swipes between two reads of the same live values: per stock, and compounded into sectors (Yahoo's own classification, cached; unknowns stay honestly "Unclassified" rather than guessed). Each card carries a **sell flag**: type the profit percentage you want and Aurum shows the exact price to sell at, how far away it is, and the money it represents — turning green the moment the price gets there. An edit button opens the position's trades — correct or delete any buy/sell and every derived number (position, P/L, reports) recomputes from the fixed ledger. New trades can be sized as a **percentage** — of your invested money on a buy, of the position itself on a sell. Any position (e.g. a test entry) can be removed from the ledger without touching the others.
- **Portfolio-aware suggestions** — one shared engine slices the book by sector and every screen reads from it, so the numbers always agree. Picks (all four tabs) and the Wealth market-pulse lists tag each suggestion against your actual holdings: "In portfolio · 12% of book", "Adds to Technology — already 45% of book", or "New sector for your book". The Wealth tab adds a "Your book" card: sector split, and number-backed notes — concentration warnings, whether this week's trending theme is already covered, and how much of the book lacks sector data (left out of sector reads rather than guessed).
- **Live market data** — quotes with day range, 52-week range and volume; 1-day, 1-week, 1-month and 3-month price charts with pinch-zoom, pan, double-tap zoom steps and a hold-for-crosshair price/time readout; powered by Yahoo Finance public endpoints (no API key). Every screen refreshes with a pull-down gesture.
- **Buy/sell advice** — RSI, moving averages, ATR-based targets and stops, plus news tone; every holding gets a hold/take-profit/cut-loss read, every watched stock gets a suggested entry price.
- **35-technique analysis** — the well-known playbook of professional desks, each with its own diagram and verdict, combined into a plain-English 5-day outlook: moving averages, RSI (with turn confirmation through the 30/70 lines), MACD, Bollinger Bands, support/resistance, fair value gaps, direction-aware Fibonacci, Ichimoku, stochastic, OBV, ADX, Donchian channel (the Turtle system), Parabolic SAR, Money Flow Index, the golden cross 50/200, Williams %R, Lambert's CCI, Keltner Channels, Chaikin Money Flow, Aroon — plus Stochastic RSI, Rate of Change, TRIX, the Ultimate Oscillator, the Vortex Indicator, Elder's Force Index, Chande Momentum, the Detrended Price Oscillator, Pring's Know Sure Thing, the Hull Moving Average, Supertrend, LeBeau's Chandelier Exit, rolling 20-day VWAP, the Accumulation/Distribution line, and monthly floor-trader pivots. Charts render as Japanese candlesticks or a line (toggle), with price gridlines, a date axis, pinch-zoom/pan, and a hold-and-drag crosshair; tapping a chart opens the full written analysis of that technique — what's drawn, the current reading, levels to watch, and a playbook.
- **Technique integrity engine** — a standalone back-tester replays the last **full year** of sessions day by day, re-runs the whole 35-technique board on each day's data only, and grades every technique against the real 5-day move that followed. The board then **re-orders by that measured record**: the top 20 techniques on that exact stock lead the screen (each with its rank number) and the rest fold away behind a toggle. Trust is statistically honest: a technique earns the **Trusted** badge only on **non-overlapping (independent) samples** — at least 10 independent calls, a 60%+ hit rate there, **and** a 5-point edge over the stock's own base drift (a rule that merely rode a rising tape is not trusted for it). Every card shows its independent record with a 95% Wilson interval and the stock's base rate beside it, the record's date range is printed, and the 5-day outlook weights each technique's vote by its own measured accuracy on that stock. No real track record, no gold.
- **Stocks** — the Watchlist tab grew into a Stocks section with two modes. **Watchlist** keeps the personal list (pins, entry advice, gold relation). **Search** answers two ways: free tag search over the whole US symbol directory, or a curated **browse by sector** — ~250 liquid names across 12 shelves (semiconductors & AI, technology, financials, healthcare, energy, consumer, media & telecom, industrials, auto & EV, materials, gold & silver, utilities & REITs) — every row shows its live price, day move, and real 2-week move, lists sort best-first, and the sector's top 2-week performers wear a gold border. The browse is **sector-rotation aware**: each shelf is ranked live against the others by its proxy ETF's 5- and 20-day momentum, volume surge, and news tone (the same half-hour scan the Wealth tab uses), the chips reorder hot → cold with a flame on the top trending shelves, and a pulse card states the selected shelf's weekly rank and whether money is flowing in, rotating out, or steady — plus what the shelf's headlines are saying. It is also **portfolio-aware**: rows you hold carry an "Held · % of book" tag and the pulse card totals the shelf's share of your book from exact holdings membership (never guessed). And it looks forward: a **next-week breakout watch** flags the shelf's names pressing their 20-day high on real volume with the week's momentum behind them — each candidate must pass measured gates (5-day move, volume vs its own average, distance to the high) and a non-bearish 35-technique board, with analyst/insider-flavored headlines quoted on the card; a shelf with no genuine setup honestly shows none. Any row stars straight into the watchlist.
- **Relations** — a Relation toggle on the Picks tab maps first-party links: each market giant (NVIDIA, AMD, Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Tesla, Exxon, Boeing) with the stocks tied to it by a documented business relationship — its manufacturer, suppliers, and the companies that build around it. When AMD rises this week, the tab answers who rises with it: groups are ordered by the giant's weekly move, and every pair shows the related stock's own week move plus the measured 3-month correlation of daily returns (a curated link that measures weak is shown weak).
- **Account-sized five-day buy plan** — a toggle on the analysis screen switches from the techniques to a staged deployment plan built from the documented playbooks of Livermore (probe then pyramid), O'Neil (50/30/20 scaling), Paul Tudor Jones (200-day MA gate, 5:1 reward-to-risk), Elder (2% risk rule), and Buffett/Munger (limit orders at value): tranches with trigger prices, an ATR-padded structural stop, targets, risk math, and a day-by-day schedule. The budget is **derived from your recorded account**: sized so a full stop-out loses at most your profile's risk-per-trade share of equity, capped by your position-size limit and tracked cash. When the app can't see your account it falls back to a $3,000 order budget and **says so explicitly** — a fixed budget is never dressed up as the 2% account rule.
- **Gold relation** — 125-day correlation of any stock against GLD: moves with gold, inverse, or unrelated.
- **News** — last 5 days of headlines per stock with sentiment dots and the price move on each news day.
- **Portfolio advisor** — the Wealth tab now opens on a portfolio-evaluation engine that reads every holding through the 35-technique board, its own support structure and ATR, the news, and the book's sector weights, then answers the four questions that matter: what to **hold** and why, what to **sell** and when, where to **cut the loss**, and what to **trim**. Every verdict card carries a one-line action, a concrete "when", measured why-bullets (P/L, board votes, RSI, distance from the 50-day, share of book), a forward take-profit level and an exit level. Below the verdicts sit an **allocation plan** (current vs suggested weight per position, with what the plan frees to cash) and a **sector rebalance**: when a sector crosses the 35% concentration line, the engine names the weakest holding in it to sell, the dollars to move, and a board-approved stock from the strongest inflowing sector to buy instead — and when no candidate passes the board, it honestly says to hold cash. A new trade invalidates the cached review, so the advice never describes a book you no longer hold.
- **Money-flow engine** — a standalone engine measures where the money and the volume are actually moving, sector by sector, with the standard institutional equations: up-day dollar-volume share, Chaikin Money Flow, the Money Flow Index, OBV slope, relative strength against the S&P 500, volume pace, member breadth, and news tone. Each sector gets a fixed-scale 0–100 flow score and an **inflow / balanced / outflow** verdict that needs at least 3 of the 4 money signals to agree — a mixed read is reported as balanced, never forced into a story. This engine drives the "Where the money is moving" card, ranks the sector-gap themes, and picks next week's sectors.
- **Next-session engine** — the most thorough scan in the app: Yahoo's eight predefined market screens (a broad liquid-name sample, not every US stock) are swept, the shortlist runs through the 35-technique board (bearish boards are out), and each finalist gets a **measured analog-day study** — the engine finds the past sessions of the last ~6 months that looked like today (same-direction move of similar size, similar close position in the range) and counts how often the next day actually closed higher and by how much. The 10 strongest picks come with a fixed-scale score, the measured follow-through probability ("after 14 similar days it closed higher 71% of the time"), an entry, a target from the measured analog average, a stop off the stock's own support, and an honest ATR range. A pick that clears **every** gate at once — score ≥ 78, measured 65%+ follow-through over ≥ 8 analog days, bullish board at 60%+ indicator agreement — is flagged as an extreme setup, and a background watcher **pushes a notification** for it after the close or pre-open, once per name per day. Fewer than 6 analog days and the probability is shown as *not measured*, never invented.
- **Market pulse** — a whole-market rating (0-100) built from measured data: S&P 500 / Nasdaq 100 / Russell 2000 trend vs their 50-day averages, breadth across hundreds of scanned liquid names, the share that advanced in the last session, and the VIX. It answers plainly whether this week is worth new money (invest / selective / defensive, with the numbers behind the call) and lists the last session's best performers (liquidity-gated).
- **The 2% desk** — a standalone tab built around one editable daily profit target (default 2.06%), answered two ways behind a toggle. **Pre-market**: the 10 strongest pre-market movers **on live pre-market prints** (prices read fresh each visit, never more than a minute stale, with the list recomputed when it ages past two minutes), each measured against the target: how often its intraday high actually rose that far above the open across ~60 sessions, what a median day really delivers, its ATR budget, how often its gap-ups held into the close, and the clock windows where its low and high have actually printed (shown in Amman time) — including a warning when the high tends to land *before* the low. **Market open**: once the session is trading, a live scan for stocks still positioned to add the target *from their current price* — every name must pass four gates: trading above its open, volume at or above its normal pace (today's volume scaled by session progress vs the 3-month average), a bullish 35-technique board, and the remaining move historically reachable (the open-to-high stretch it requires, counted across ~60 sessions, leads the ranking). Outside the regular session the scan says so instead of dressing stale numbers as live ones.
- **Sector gaps** — the Wealth tab answers one question a week: where is the portfolio thin, and what should be bought. Themes are ranked by the **money-flow engine** (a theme the money is measurably leaving gets no new cash), measured against what is actually held (real sector weights where the mapping is unambiguous, exact holdings membership for cross-sector themes like AI or quantum), and marked **Missing / Light / Covered / Heavy** with the flow score on the row. Each theme proposes the **3 strongest stocks from its full sector shelf** (up to 14 liquid names, not a fixed short list) — ranked on measured evidence: the last 3 trading days' move, the latest session's volume against its 20-day average, the 35-technique board, RSI, and the last 5 days of headlines, with the freshest report-style headline shown on the card. Extended names get a pullback entry instead of a chase. The split is expressed in percentages of new money — the user decides the dollars. Every Wealth section has its own fold toggle, so the tab shows only the parts you want open.
- **Next week** — built Thursday through Monday, the preview admits only sectors with a confirmed measured inflow, screens their liquid names, and proposes up to 5 for Monday with entry, target, stop, R:R, and the flow-backed reason — sized against the book's buying power, or as percentages when the book is empty. When no sector clears the money-flow gate, the engine returns an explicit cash plan instead of forcing a buy list.
- **U-pattern day trading** — the Picks tab opens on a standalone engine built around one intraday shape: the stocks that habitually dip after the open and climb back through the close. It scans Yahoo's eight predefined market screens (a broad liquid sample, liquidity-gated), then fingerprints each candidate from ~21 sessions of 5-minute bars: a session counts as a U-day only when four measured legs agree — the low printed before 13:00 ET, the close landed in the top 30% of the range, the rebound off the low was ≥1%, and an OLS parabola fitted to the day's closes actually curves upward (the mathematical U). The exact buy rule the app signals live — *after a ≥0.8% dip, buy when a 5-minute bar closes back above VWAP without a new session low; exit 15:45 ET* — is replayed over every fingerprint session, wins and losses alike, and its record (fired / won / average return) is printed on the card. Hard gates keep the list honest: ≥40% U-rate, ≥1% median rebound, a rule that actually paid ≥55% of the time with positive expectancy, and a non-bearish 35-technique board — a day where nothing qualifies shows nothing. During the session every pick is tracked live through a state machine — no dip yet → in the dip (wait) → **BUY ZONE** (turn confirmed: entry, stop under the session low, target from the measured median rebound, R:R) → too late (don't chase) — and a 15-minute background watcher **pushes a notification the moment a name enters its buy zone** (once per name per day, never after 15:30 ET). Each card carries the name's own dip/sell clock windows in Amman time, its U-rate, its parabola agreement count, and its rule record — a tendency with a measured history, never a promise.
- **Best entries** — an Entries toggle on the Picks tab sweeps Yahoo's eight predefined screens (hundreds of liquid names: most actives, gainers, losers, undervalued large caps and growth, small caps — a broad sample, not every US stock; the screen shows exactly how many screens and rows each scan covered) and ranks the 10 stocks sitting at the best entry price right now: long trend intact above the 200-day average, pulled back toward the 50-day and clustered support, RSI reset, honest reward/risk to the nearest resistance, and a 35-technique board that does not read the dip as a falling knife. Each card shows the entry (or a patient limit), target, stop, upside and risk percentages, and the numbers behind the read.
- **Power hour** — a Power toggle on the Picks tab for the overnight-momentum play: buy in the last 90 minutes of the session (2:30–4:00 PM ET — the app shows the window in your local time), sell into tomorrow morning's strength. The screener universe is swept for names finishing near their daily high on hot volume after 4 strong trading days (total move, up-day consistency, acceleration into today), each confirmed by the 35-technique board, with an honest ATR-based next-day potential range, a morning exit target, and a hard stop under the day's low.
- **Weekly picks** — every Monday (and on every refresh) the app ranks the 10 strongest setups from an 85-name universe widened with live market-wide movers from Yahoo's screens (most actives, gainers, growth), plus a separate under-$25 watch list of 5, each with a one-line data-backed reason and full analysis.
- **Bank sync** — a notification listener captures Bank al Etihad trade alerts (English and Arabic), parses side/symbol/quantity/price/currency/broker reference, and imports them into the portfolio after your review. Unattended auto-import demands more than a confident parse: every field present, a **broker transaction reference**, USD denomination, and no identical trade already in the ledger — anything less becomes a review notification instead of a silent write. **Non-USD alerts require an explicit FX conversion** in the review form, and the original currency + rate are stored on the trade.
- **Reports** — daily, weekly, and monthly summaries of trades done: realized P/L, buys/sells totals, best and worst trade. Every trade listed in a report is editable in place — tap it to correct the side, shares, price, fees, or the trade date (a date change re-files it under the right day, week, and month), or delete it; positions, P/L, and all reports recompute from the fixed ledger. A sell's **realized outcome (+/-) can be pinned** to the broker's real number when it differs from the computed one — pinned outcomes flow through the position, the summary, and every report, and are labeled so they never read as computed.
- **Ledger truth (v6)** — the trade forms **reject oversells** (you can't record selling shares the ledger doesn't hold; edits are replay-validated too), the dashboard headline is honestly named **Holdings value**, holdings with no live quote are flagged "at cost — no price" instead of faking a flat price, and prices older than 15 minutes carry an "as of" stamp. An optional **cash ledger** records deposits, withdrawals, dividends, interest, and fees — once you track it, the dashboard shows cash and true total equity. **Stock splits** are first-class ledger rows, and every trade can carry a note (your thesis at entry) plus the original currency and FX rate when it came from a non-USD bank alert.
- **Wallet, liquidity & P/L (v6.5)** — one number you state (the capital you put in) drives the whole cash picture, and it is tied to the ledger by a single identity: **liquidity = wallet − invested + realized P/L**. A sell hands back the cost basis of the shares *and* the profit or loss booked on them, so closing a winner raises your liquidity by the full proceeds instead of only the money you originally paid — and a closed position keeps funding your cash after it has left the book. The Portfolio hero shows wallet, invested, liquidity, **realized**, **total P/L** and **net worth** together, with the formula printed under them; the Reports card names the realized share of the cash. **Net worth reconciles two ways** (liquidity + holdings, and wallet + total P/L) — unit-tested so the readings can never drift apart. When the ledger has more deployed than your stated wallet covers, the shortfall is shown honestly in red rather than floored at $0. The same liquidity feeds the Wealth tab's deployment plan and the account-sized buy plan — and a fully deployed account is told plainly that funding a new plan means selling something first. Unrealized P/L never moves your liquidity — a gain sits on paper until you sell, and only the sell converts it to spendable cash (locked by a test sweeping prices from $0.01 to $100,000). Every wallet figure prints to the cent at any size, so a balance is never rounded down to hide money you hold.
- **Investor profile (v6)** — Settings holds your policy: time horizon, risk tolerance, risk-per-trade, position cap, sector cap. Every personalized output — verdicts, trims, loss rules, profit rules, buy-plan sizing — derives from it and **cites it**; until you set it, the app uses balanced defaults and labels them as defaults.
- **Company research (v6)** — every stock detail carries fundamentals from Yahoo's quoteSummary: sector/industry/profile, revenue and margins, cash/debt/FCF/ROE, valuation multiples with mechanical **bull/base/bear EPS×multiple scenarios** (formula disclosed, never a single "fair value"), analyst consensus **with count and target dispersion**, and the dated catalysts (next earnings — flagged when within a week — ex-dividend and payment dates). Missing fields render as "—", never as silent zeros.
- **Performance & risk (v6)** — the Wealth tab reconstructs your **actual equity curve** by replaying the ledger against daily closes: time-weighted return vs SPY over the same days, annualized volatility, max drawdown, beta, Sharpe (rf 0, labeled), and the correlated pairs in your book that only look diversified. Window and method are printed with the numbers.
- **Data honesty (v6)** — "no data", "stale data", and "fetch failed" are never conflated: news says "no headlines (feed checked)" vs "couldn't load news", scans say "no setup found across N screens / M rows" vs "scan FAILED — source unreachable", analysis distinguishes short listing history from a failed fetch, and the market pulse **refuses to make a call** (INCOMPLETE) when too few of its inputs were actually measured. Every scan header shows its universe coverage.
- **Price alerts (v6)** — watch any level on any symbol (one tap pre-fills the advice's own target or stop); a background sweep checks every ~15 minutes and notifies once per alert.
- **Advice history (v6)** — every Wealth verdict is logged immutably (action, price at the time, engine version) and the history screen shows what the price did since — wins and misses alike.
- **Privacy & durability (v6)** — optional app lock behind your device screen lock, JSON **export/restore** of the full ledger (merge-safe, never duplicates), configurable retention that auto-deletes raw bank captures, one-tap delete of all captures, and a versioned in-app **Methodology & disclosures** center covering data sources, latency, score definitions, backtest limitations, and risks.

## Install

Scan with your phone camera to download the latest APK directly:

<img src="install-qr.png" alt="Scan to install Aurum" width="260" />

Or download `aurum.apk` from the [latest release](../../releases/latest). Open it on your Android phone (allow "Install unknown apps"), then:

1. Open **Settings → Bank sync → Enable** to grant Notification Access (needed for bank-alert capture).
2. Add your first trade with the gold **+** button, or let a bank notification arrive and import it from the **Feed** tab.

Requires Android 8.0+ and an internet connection for market data.

## Build from source

```
gradlew.bat :app:assembleDebug
```

JDK 17+, Android SDK 34. See `CONTRACTS.md` for the module architecture.

## Disclaimer

All suggestions are computed from public historical market data and news headlines. They are decision support, not financial advice.
